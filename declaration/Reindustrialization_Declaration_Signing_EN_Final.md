# Declaration of Reindustrialization

## A Joint Statement by Free Individuals

We, ordinary citizens of the United States, of Japan, and of the wider free world—together with those in China and beyond who hold the same convictions—now join our voices in common purpose.

## We hold these beliefs:

- That the right to pursue happiness rests on the freedom to do real, dignified, and productive work.
- That a free people must hold in its own hands the capacities most essential to its life—not only in time of war, but in time of peace.
- That free societies cannot endure on money and policy alone; they depend on generation after generation who take pride in making things well, and who hold craftsmanship to be a matter of honor.
- That cooperation among free societies must leave each of them more independent, never more beholden to another.
- That to place our most vital productive capacity in the hands of regimes or entities that honor neither individual rights nor predictable rules is to forge invisible shackles for ourselves.

Today, the United States, together with like-minded partners, is rebuilding genuine capacity in semiconductors, critical minerals, energy, shipbuilding, advanced computing infrastructure, and other sectors truly vital to a nation's security and its people's livelihood. This direction, and these principles, we support—as the work of no single administration, and as a task far from finished.

And we oppose any course that leaves free societies ever more dependent, in their core capabilities, upon those who violate the principles set forth above.

## We pledge:

- As individuals, to support the reindustrialization of the United States, and a freedom-grounded reindustrialization in our own nations.
- To refuse to surrender strategic productive capacity to any regime or entity that systematically violates these principles.
- To advance cooperation among free societies that is predictable in substance and predictable in manner, reciprocal in the timing and design of its institutions as well as in their eventual outcome, and that genuinely strengthens the independence of every party to it.
- To teach the generation that follows us that freedom without the capacity to produce—and to compute—what matters most will always be fragile; and to invest patiently in the people, the apprenticeships, and the craftsmanship that such capacity ultimately requires.
- To recognize, as Hamilton taught, that the essentials of national supply and the public credit stand together; that support for reindustrialization is inseparable from support for fiscal discipline.

To these words we pledge our names and our honor.

**Signed this Fourth Day of July, 2026**, the Two Hundred and Fiftieth Anniversary of the Declaration of Independence of the United States of America, in the City of Philadelphia, Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, United States of America, at the historic home of the First Troop Philadelphia City Cavalry (the First City Troop), in the birthplace of the nation.
